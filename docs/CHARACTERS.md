# Characters: how they're built (plan)

## The way now (2026-11-16, the owner's call)

Everything below this section is the earlier plan, set aside by the owner on 2026-11-16 in favour
of this:

1. **The model:** generated by the owner on their own Hugging Face space (TRELLIS.2, with a blank
   face), painted.
2. **The skeleton:** the owner rigs it in AccuRig, fingers placed by hand, and exports for Unity.
   That export loses the paint.
3. **The paint back:** `scripts/blender/retexture_rig.py` puts the painted model's pictures on the
   rigged one (the UVs are the same), squares up the skeleton the Unity export turns a quarter over,
   and writes a GLB. To keep the paint in AccuRig instead, give it an FBX or OBJ with the pictures
   beside it or packed in.
4. **Two copies:** `captain-full.glb` keeps all ~490,000 triangles for cutscenes and close-ups,
   packed small with meshopt and WebP (`scripts/blender/game_copy.py`, `scripts/pack-glb.mjs`);
   `captain-raid.glb` is cut down for raids.
5. **The face:** no change to the mesh. Patches shaped to the sockets, brow and mouth carry eyes,
   lids, brows and a mouth drawn in the shader, driven by springs: expressions, blinks, glances,
   and lip sync from text or from sound (`scripts/blender/face_map.py`, `render/face/`,
   `logic/face/`).
6. **Movement:** the owner's stock clips, converted to this skeleton (`docs/ANIMATION.md`).
7. **Hands:** every finger bends on its own, three joints each, eased; the bend's axis is worked
   out at rest from the bones, so a fist, a point or a loose hand is just a number per finger
   (`render/captain.ts`).
8. **Hair in the breeze:** set aside by the owner (2026-11-22): moving the sculpted hair's points
   tore it into jagged bits up close (the Captain's crown at the console) and cost every frame.
   The hair is still now, and so is the block figures' (their swinging ponytails too).

In the game since 2026-11-16 (since 2026-11-22 a hero of his own, not in the Rifleman's place; see
DESIGN.md): the Captain stands aboard the boat with his face, the stock idle and his hands; picked, he says his line and eases his hands, and
on Ready closes them into fists. He plays Steele's part in the cutscenes too: his own face says each
line, lips in time with the words, his expression set by the line's mood (`MOOD_FACE`). In a raid
he wears his block figure's moves, a pistol in each hand (`render/captainRaid.ts`). Standing aboard and in cutscenes he's on
the stock idle (the clips are in the repo since 2026-11-16); without them he stands at ease on a
pose of his own: arms down, breathing, his weight shifting.
Offline, where his model can't be fetched, his block figure stands in his place.
`models-wip/debug/captain/captain.html` shows him under a still camera.

### The Rifleman, the Shaman and Professor Terror (2026-11-21)

The owner remade the Rifleman and the Shaman and made the old Professor Terror, rigged in AccuRig on
the Captain's skeleton, blank-faced as he was. Each went through the Captain's pipeline:

1. `scripts/blender/retexture_rig.py`: the paint back on the rigged FBX (the UVs matched to within
   0.0000005 on all three).
2. A full copy for close-ups (`game_copy.py`, every triangle, pictures at 2048, AccuRig's face
   shapes dropped), packed with meshopt: `public/models/rifleman-full.glb` 3.4 MB,
   `shaman-full.glb` 5.3 MB, `terror-full.glb` 4.9 MB.
3. A light copy for afar (`bake_game.py`), done as the owner's walkthrough has it: thinned to
   8,000 triangles, its faces turned outward, unwrapped afresh, colour and normals baked from the
   full model at 2048 in Cycles; about 1 MB each (`rifleman.glb`, `shaman.glb`, `terror.glb`). A
   few small flecks of skin show through on the Rifleman's sleeves where the thinned mesh bridges
   the armpit; it's only drawn when he's small on the screen.
4. Their faces mapped (`face_map.py`): eye sockets, brow ridges and mouths found on the blank
   faces. The Shaman's sculpted face hid his sockets from the finder (it took his temples); his are
   given by hand (`eyes=0.024,0.885,0.905`: the deepest point of the socket and the brow ridge's
   most forward, down a column through the eye; the first hand-placing put his eyes up on the
   ridge), and the finder now looks only on bare skin. Each eye's patch is wide enough for the
   whole brow: narrower ones had cut the three faces' brows off square, like bars.

**Eyes by human proportions (2026-11-22/23, the owner's call: "match them to what humans look
like"):** each face's eyes were placed by the human canon (an eye a fifth of the face across at the
cheekbones, about 0.13 of the head from chin to crown, one eye's width between them), then trimmed
by the owner's eye (the Captain's a bit big, the Rifleman's a bit small and low) and by a second
opinion the owner asked for (Antigravity, shown the four faces front on; its answers are in
`models-wip/debug/faces/`). After the second round it called the Captain and the Shaman fine. What
each map is made with (`face_map.py`, `eyes=<height>,<brow>` `place=<eye width>,<middle from the
nose>`, model units):

| Face             | eyes          | place          |
| ---------------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| The Captain      | 0.8358, 0.860 | 0.0335, 0.036  |
| The Rifleman     | 0.8857, 0.899 | 0.023, 0.024   |
| The Shaman       | 0.8875, 0.905 | 0.0207, 0.0224 |
| Professor Terror | 0.9039, 0.918 | 0.022, 0.0238  |

The Rifleman's ran onto the sides of his wide nose bridge (the owner, 2026-11-23), and a third round
settled it the human way: smaller eyes, not further apart. His face is five equal widths across
(cheek, eye, bridge, eye, cheek), the inner corners just clear of the bridge's edge, and his brows
start there too (`brows=0.0127`, the map's `browStart`). The eye itself is drawn as a person's
(`render/face/faceShader.ts`): an almond about a third as tall as it's wide, the upper lid highest
towards the nose and over the top fifth of the iris, the lower lid shallow and lowest towards the
ear, the outer corner a touch higher, an iris two fifths of the eye across. The brows are hair, not
painted bars: thickest at the head, which fades in over the root of the nose, a low arch over the
outer third, tapering to a fine tail level with the head, each hair its own shade.

In the game (`render/captain.ts`, `loadPerson`) each is the Captain's equal: the full model up
close and the light one afar on one skeleton, the stock clips, every finger bending on its own, a
face drawn on in their own colours (brows and lashes from their hair, the iris, the lips) that

all four, with their faces, expressions, hands and hair.

### Building them from scratch (Proposed, 2026-11-22)

Claude's judgement against the owner's bar: the way now gets the faces, fingers and textures there,
but it can't get the hair or the faces all the way to a $50 standard, so by the owner's own
instruction ("if the models don't look good, abandon how we make them and plan to build them from
scratch") this is the way forward. It waits on the owner's go-ahead, since it's about a week a
character; until each new one is done, the current model stands in.

The way now starts from a generated model (one sculpted surface, its paint, AccuRig's skeleton) and
adds to it in the game. Its limits are the generated mesh's: the hair is one shell, so it can only
bend, not part into strands; the face is painted on, not shaped; the topology is uneven, so it
creases badly at the elbows and knees. If the owner judges them short of the bar on the phone, the
plan is to keep the generated model only as the reference, and build each character again in
Blender, the Captain first (the most seen), then the Shaman, Terror and the Rifleman:

1. **Body, by retopology** over the generated model: clean quad loops round the eyes, mouth,
   shoulders, elbows and knees, about 20,000 to 30,000 triangles for close-ups and 6,000 to 8,000
   for raids. The generated sculpt's detail is baked into normal maps, as `bake_game.py` does now.
2. **Face, as shapes:** the ARKit 52 face shapes (blinks, brows, jaw, lips, cheeks) sculpted on the
   new head, with eyes as their own spheres with lids. three.js plays these as morph targets, and the
   face rig drives them from the same expressions, blinks and lip sync it drives the painted
   features with now.
3. **Hair, as cards:** locks as separate strips on a few chains of bones, swung by spring physics
   in the game (like the old block figures' `SpringChains`), so they part and lift, not only bend.
   Tried on the generated models first (2026-11-22): their hair cut automatically into eight to ten
   locks round the head, each on a spring chain. The locks swung well (the Rifleman's lifted in a
   gust, the Shaman's streamed as he turned), but the generated hair is many thin overlapping
   shells, not clean locks, so it tore where the locks met at the crown and loose strands stuck out
   on long levers. It was taken out again. Clean chunks have to be made as chunks. Then (the same day)
   locks grown over the Captain's sculpted hair in code: tapered tubes rooted on his scalp, combed
   back along its shape, each on a spring chain. They swung without tearing, but sank into the
   sculpt and showed only their tips, like little horns. Locks good enough for the bar are shaped
   by hand in Blender, one by one, round the head they belong to: that's this step as it has to be
   done, and it's artist's work more than code. A third try slimmed his sculpted hair to a cap
   at load and laid the locks over it: the packed model can't be reshaped at load without its
   shading breaking, and the locks read as wiry curls, not his chunky swept quiff. Three ways in code
   (`models-wip/debug/heroes2/`) have now been tried and set aside.
4. **Clothes, as their own pieces** over the body, with seams, collars, cuffs and pockets modelled.
   They're painted in Blender (or Substance Painter) with fabric that has a weave, stitching and
   wear, at 4096 for close-ups and 1024 for raids.
5. **Hands:** five-fingered with three joints each, weighted by hand: the Captain's `Hands` drives
   them as it does now.
6. **The same skeleton** (AccuRig's CC_Base), so the stock clips, the raid moves and everything in
   `render/captain.ts` carries over.

It's about a week of work a character, against the hour or so the way now takes, and it's work
done in Blender, not in an engine (see below).

### The brief for the hair and faces (2026-11-22)

The owner doesn't model in Blender. Whoever does this (an artist on commission, or the owner
following a guide written from it), each character's delivery is:

1. **The file:** one `.blend` or `.fbx` with the character on its present AccuRig skeleton
   (`CC_Base_*` bones, unchanged names and rest pose), in the same size and place as
   `models-wip/heroes2/<name>-rigged.glb`, which is the reference to start from.
2. **The hair:** the sculpted hair replaced by 8 to 20 separate locks, chunky and in the shape of
   the concept (the Captain's swept-back quiff, the Rifleman's swept black hair, the Shaman's long
   white locks and beard, Terror's swept-back grey hair). Each lock is closed (no open edges), sits
   on a hair-coloured cap over the scalp, and hangs from its own chain of 3 or 4 bones under
   `CC_Base_Head`, named `hair<lock>_<n>` (`hair1_1`, `hair1_2`, ...), weighted smoothly from root
   to tip. The game's springs (`render/springBones.ts`) swing chains named that way as they are.
3. **The face:** the blank face given eyes (separate spheres with lids), a mouth and brows in the
   concept's style, and the 52 ARKit face shapes (eyeBlinkLeft, jawOpen, mouthSmileLeft, browInnerUp
   and the rest) as shape keys on the head. The face rig (`render/face/`) will drive those in place
   of the painted features.
4. **The fingers:** unchanged: AccuRig's three joints a finger already bend separately.
5. **Pictures:** the clothes and skin painted at 4096 with the fabric's weave, stitching and wear
   (colour, roughness and normal maps); the hair's with strands running along each lock.
6. **Size:** about 30,000 triangles in all, hair included.

What happens to it here: `retexture_rig.py` is no longer needed; `game_copy.py` and `bake_game.py`
make the full and light copies as now; the face rig is pointed at the shape keys; the springs pick
up the hair. Roughly: hair $30 to $80 and face shapes $50 to $150 a character on commission, or
a long weekend each following a guide.



Neither would make these models better, and both would cost the game.

- **Making them:** neither engine models, rigs or paints a character. That's done in Blender,
  AccuRig and the owner's generator whichever engine runs the game, and it's where the quality
  comes from.
- **Running them:** Unreal 5's characters are its strength (MetaHuman faces, groom hair), but
  MetaHumans are realistic, not this style. Unreal builds a native iPhone app, not this web game,
  so it would mean rewriting everything and leaving the PWA. Godot 4 can export to the web, but
  only on its compatibility renderer, with a 30 to 40 MB download and threading limits on iPhone
  Safari, for no gain in faces, fingers or hair over what three.js does here.
- **What would lift them further, in this pipeline:** sculpted face shapes (ARKit's 52) made in
  Blender in place of painted features; hair as separate cards on spring bones; 4096 pictures for
  close-ups. All of these load into three.js as they are.

## The bar (the owner's words, 2026-11-14)

"$50 game ready": what a character sold for about $50 on an asset store has. A model passes only
when every line here is true, checked from pictures before it goes in the game:

- **Looks like the design:** the same chunky, smooth, toy-like style, so it sits with the rest of
  the game.
- **A clean surface:** four-sided faces in loops round the eyes, the mouth and every joint, so it
  bends and smiles without creasing. About 30,000 to 40,000 triangles for cutscenes and the hero
  screen, about 8,000 to 10,000 for raids.
- **Separate pieces on one skeleton:** body, each eyeball, teeth, tongue, brows, beard, every clump
  of hair, every piece of clothing, every prop (toothpick, watch, necklace, bracelets, beads,
  feather).
- **Emotions:** happy, sad, angry, scared, surprised, a smirk, plus the mouth shapes of speech, as
  face shapes the game blends between.
- **Eyes that blink and look**, each on its own.
- **Fingers that move and clasp separately:** three bones in every finger and thumb.
- **Hair in chunks that blow in the breeze:** 12 to 20 clumps, each on its own chain of bones.
- **Interesting textures:** skin with warmth and blush, cloth with a weave and stitching, grained
  leather and wood, a crystal that glows; not flat paint.

## Verdict on what was made (2026-11-14)

The Captain and the Shaman were generated whole (TRELLIS.2, about 185,000 and 191,000 triangles).
Close pictures are in `models-wip/look/` (`s11-look.py` makes them). They don't reach the bar, and
can't be brought to it:

- **The faces are soft copies of the designs.** Eyes are smudged paint on a flat cheek (the
  Shaman's came out pale and blank), the mouth is a dent, the beard is a blur, the Shaman's beads
  and the Captain's watch are melted lumps. The owner's words: "the shaman's eyes look weird and
  same with the captain".
- **The surface is a scan-like skin of small triangles with no loops.** A mouth can't smile and a
  lid can't close on it without tearing or creasing, so emotions are out of reach however the
  pieces round it are built.
- **Everything is one fused shell:** hair, clothes and skin. Hair can't swing (tried on the Medic:
  it tore), clothes can't move apart from the body.
- **Triangles in the wrong places:** 185,000 of them, spent evenly, where a built model spends a
  fifth as many on purpose.

Building eyes, lids and hair as separate pieces on top of the generated body (the second try, same
day) fixes the eyes and the hair but not the mouth, the emotions, the fingers' skin or the clothes.
It was stopped half way.

## Godot or Unreal? (the owner's question, 2026-11-14)

Neither would make the models better. Both are **engines**: they show and animate a model, as
three.js does here. The model itself is made in a modelling tool (Blender, ZBrush, Maya) whichever
engine shows it, and Blender is installed and already runs from scripts for the buildings.

- **Unreal** has one character maker, MetaHuman. It makes realistic people, not this toy style, and
  far too heavy for a phone's browser. Whether a MetaHuman may ship outside Unreal is disputed
  ([allowed, June 2025](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/06/you-can-now-sell-metahumans-or-use-them-in-unity-or-godot/);
  [still tied, 2026](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/metahuman-2026-web-app-shutdown-migration-guide)).
  Unreal also can't make a web game: it dropped browser export in 4.24, and Unreal 5 only streams
  video from a server
  ([summary](https://www.summerengine.com/de/make-great-games/unreal-engine-html5-export)). Moving
  there means rewriting the game as an App Store app.
- **Godot** can export to the web, but has no character-making tools, and what it offers for
  characters (skeletons, face shapes, spring bones for hair) this game already has in three.js.
  Moving would cost a rewrite and gain nothing for the models.
- **Worth borrowing:** how MetaHuman splits a character into pieces, and Apple's ARKit list of 52
  face shapes, which is the standard list studios build emotions from.

## The from-scratch way

All in Blender (5.2, installed), driven by scripts in `scripts/blender/` like the buildings, so a
character can be rebuilt after any change. The owner sees pictures at each **check**.

1. **A test head first (the gate).** Two free, professionally made bases, tried side by side on the
   Captain's head:
   - **MakeHuman for Blender (MPFB 2.0.18).** A body with clean loops, real separate fingers, a
     skeleton with finger bones, separate eyes, teeth, tongue, brows and lashes, **the 52 ARKit
     face shapes** and speech mouth shapes ready-made. Bodies and face shapes are CC0
     ([MPFB](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/mpfb/),
     [asset packs](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks.html),
     [face shapes, 2.0.16](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/releases/release_2016.html)).
     Made for realistic people, so it has to be reshaped to the toy style: the risk.
   - **Blender Studio's Human Base Meshes (1.4.1, CC0).** Has a stylized man and a stylized head
     already close to the designs' style, but no skeleton and no face shapes: those would be built
     ([bundle](https://www.blender.org/download/demo/asset-bundles/human-base-meshes/human-base-meshes-bundle-v1.2.0.zip),
     [notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/features/asset_system/asset_bundles/human_base_meshes/)).

   Each is reshaped by script to the Captain's head (bigger head and eyes, block nose, strong jaw),
   given eyes, brows, beard and hair clumps, and rendered close: neutral, blink, smile, angry,
   talking. **Check:** the owner picks one, or says neither. If neither reaches the bar, stop: what's
   left is a person sculpting (the owner in Blender, or a hired artist) or a bought base model.
2. **Body and proportions.** The chosen base reshaped to the design's measurements (head about a
   quarter of the height, broad chest, thick limbs, big hands and feet), with the same reshaping
   applied to every face shape so they still work.
3. **The face.** Eyeballs with a painted iris and a dark rim; lids that close; teeth, tongue and a
   dark inside to the mouth; brows and beard as their own pieces. The 52 face shapes kept, and a
   short list named for the game (blink left and right, brows up, down and inner, smile, frown, jaw
   open, the speech shapes). **Check:** a sheet of every emotion.
4. **Hair.** Each clump drawn as a curve from root to tip and given thickness and a taper by
   script: about 12 for the Captain's swept quiff, about 18 for the Shaman's long hair, with his
   beads and feather hung on theirs. Each clump gets a chain of three or four bones, which the game
   already swings (`render/springBones.ts`).
5. **Clothes and props, each its own piece,** built over the body: shirt with collar, buttons and
   rolled cuffs; trousers or cargo shorts with pockets and belt loops; belt and buckle; boat shoes
   or sandals; watch, bracelets, necklace and crystal, toothpick. Hems get thickness so they read
   as cloth. Skin hidden under clothes is removed so nothing pokes through.
6. **Textures.** Painted by procedure and baked to one 2048 sheet a character (1024 for raids):
   skin with a warm gradient and blush on cheeks, nose and knuckles; linen weave on the shirts,
   twill on the trousers, stitching along the seams; grained leather; wood grain on the beads;
   brushed metal; hair with flow lines; the crystal with a glow inside. Shading baked in the folds,
   and a bump map for the fine detail. Compressed for phones.
7. **Skeleton and weights,** in Blender: the game's bone names, three bones a finger, twist bones
   in the limbs, a jaw, eye bones, the hair chains. Clothes take their weights from the body under
   them. **Check:** a sheet of hard poses (a fist, each finger alone, pointing, arms overhead, a
   deep squat, head turned and tilted), looked at before anything goes in the game.
8. **Into the game.** Two copies, as now. The animator's face moves from bones to blending face
   shapes: moods, blinking and the mouth of each line. **Check:** a cutscene on the owner's phone.
9. **The rest of the cast the same way:** the Shaman after the Captain, then each hero from the
   designs already in `docs/concept/tpose/`.

### Step 1, as it went (2026-11-14)

The owner agreed to the downloads and asked for three heads to pick from: MakeHuman realistic,
MakeHuman reshaped to the designs, and Blender Studio's stylized base. All three were made by one
script (`models-wip/debug/s13-heads.py`, untracked) and given the same Captain's dressing, built by
the script on whatever head it's handed: blond hair as 13 clumps over a short cap, a beard that
lies on the face and moves with it, block brows. Pictures are in `models-wip/look/`
(`heads-compare.jpg` has all three, six expressions each). The dressing was fitted on the stylized
head first, so it sits less well on MakeHuman's (the hair's short sides don't reach).

- **MakeHuman, realistic:** works from a script start to finish. Eyes, teeth, tongue, brows, lashes
  and a photo skin come with it, and the 52 face shapes work at once (blink, smile, talking with
  teeth showing). It looks like what it is: a realistic man, not the designs.
- **MakeHuman, reshaped:** its own sliders (square head, wide jaw, big eyes) and a warp that grows
  the eyes with every face shape still land between the two styles: realistic nose, lips and ears
  under cartoon hair. Getting from there to the designs would mean resculpting most of the face.
- **Blender Studio's stylized head:** in the designs' style as it comes. It has eyelids, lips and
  the inside of a mouth, all four-sided faces, marked by region, but nothing that moves them. Face
  shapes were built on it by script the same afternoon: blink, wide, squint, jaw open, smile, frown,
  brows up and down, an "oo", named as ARKit names them. Lids lowered a fifth give the Captain's
  calm look.
- **Its full body** (`GEO-body_male_stylized`, 12,502 points, every face four-sided, closed) has the
  same head, and marks every finger and toe segment as its own region, which is what finger weights
  need. It stands with its arms down at an angle, so it has to be raised to a T before rigging.

Where the downloads are: MakeHuman's add-on is installed in Blender itself (the owner's Blender
profile, with its assets beside it: about 330 MB), and both bundles are kept in
`models-wip/bases/` (about 380 MB, never committed).

### How a head is made (found 2026-11-15)

The owner looked at the three heads: Blender Studio's was "decent", but its face wasn't the
drawing's, the nose was wrong, and the stand-in hair and beard looked strange. They asked for the
face to be changed to the drawing, and for the beard to be drawn and extruded, not laid on. Three
ways were tried.

- **Hand-set numbers** (wider jaw by this much, a nose this long): slow, and it kept missing. Kept
  only as `models-wip/debug/make_character.hand-tuned.py`.
- **Wrapping the clean base onto the drawing's shape: this is what works.** The generated model was
  no use as a model, but it has the drawing's head in the round. The base's skin is drawn onto its
  surface a little at a time, the pull shared between neighbours so the skin keeps its even weave.
  Eyelids, eye sockets, ears, lips and the inside of the mouth aren't pulled: they ride along, so
  they still work after. The jaw is first stretched down to the design's chin, or neck skin ends up
  drawn over the chin. Out comes the drawing's square jaw, brow and wedge of a nose, on a surface
  that can still blink and talk.
- **Reading the flat drawings** for what the 3D shape gets wrong or muddies. Its eyes sit closer
  together than the drawing's, so eye spacing, eye size and the mouth's height are read off the
  front drawing. Its colours are muddy (beard and shaded skin nearly alike), so the front and side
  drawings, laid over the head by the eyes, say where beard, brow and skin are and what colour.

What comes out (the Captain, in the game's engine since 2026-11-15: `public/models/captain-hi.glb`,
about 47,000 triangles, 1 MB, 50 bones):

- **Head:** the wrapped base, with 22 **face shapes** made by the script and named as ARKit names
  them: blink, wide, squint, jaw, smile, frown, stretch, cheeks, brows, "oh" and "oo".
- **Beard, brows and the short hair of the sides are the face itself** (the owner's call,
  2026-11-15: "can't you just extrude the face out a bit and do some coloring? that way it fits
  properly"). The skin is raised a little where the drawing has them and they're painted into the
  head's picture. As separate pieces they sat wrong and showed their edges; as part of the skin
  nothing can.
- **The head's picture** (1024, `paint_face`) is painted clean, not copied from the drawing: plain
  skin with a blush, and beard, brows and short hair filled in only where each can grow, so the
  ears and cheeks never get hair colour on them. The drawings give the outlines and the strokes in
  the beard. Copying the drawing's own pixels brought its shading and smudges with it.
- **Long hair:** the design's own, cut free. Still one piece: splitting it into chunks that swing
  is next.
- **Body:** the base stood as the design stands and sized between the design's joints, then its
  bare parts (neck, forearms) wrapped onto the design. Hands keep the base's fingers, three bones
  each, made thick like the drawing's.
- **Clothes** (shirt, trousers, belt, shoes, watch): the design's own surface, cut free by colour
  and by where on him they are, thinned, and moved by the bones of the skin nearest them. Skin
  under clothes is left out of the model.
- **Eyes** (balls on bones, with a picture of an iris), **teeth**, **tongue**.
- `scripts/pack-character.mjs` packs it; in the game `render/faceShapes.ts` and the animator set
  the face's shapes for blinking, talking and moods.

Not done yet: hair in chunks that swing, the toothpick, a lighter copy for raids, the Shaman, and
finer work on the face (the blink creases, the brows are flatter than drawn, the long hair's lower
edge is ragged).

Two things that cost hours and are worth knowing:

- A face shape made with `shape_key_add(from_mix=False)` starts switched fully on. Every one has
  to be set to 0, or anything measured from the face afterwards is measured with the jaw open and
  every expression on at once.
- A mesh read back from Blender after smoothing has no directions (normals) worth trusting: they
  have to be worked out from its faces.

## What's kept and what's dropped

- **Kept, in the game:** fingers that close one at a time, blink timing and darting eyes, brows by
  mood, spring-bone hair, mouths in time with the words (`render/heroAnimator.ts`,
  `springBones.ts`, `lipSync.ts`). They drive the new models as they are, or through face shapes.
- **Kept, as moulds:** the generated Captain and Shaman (`models-wip/*-raw.glb`). The clean base
  is wrapped onto their shape and their hair is cut out of them (`character_target.py`).
- **Dropped for story characters:** using a generated model as the model. The two unfinished
  scripts that cut one up (`character_parts.py`, `hair_clumps.py`) are deleted;
  `scripts/rig-character.mjs` too: `make_character.py` and `pack-character.mjs` took its place.
- **Unchanged for now:** the seven heroes' generated bodies, until each is rebuilt in step 9.

## How studios do it (research, 2026-11-14)

- **The order of work:** a design sheet, a rough block-out, a detailed sculpt, a clean surface
  rebuilt over it (retopology), unwrapping, baked textures, rig, export. Loops round the mouth,
  elbows, knees and shoulders are what make a face and a body bend well
  ([Game-Ace](https://game-ace.com/blog/3d-character-modeling/),
  [80 Level](https://80.lv/articles/001agt-stylized-character-art-workflow-from-blockout-to-render),
  [Gnomon](https://gnomon.thegnomonworkshop.com/blog/modeling-a-stylized-character-for-games)).
  Starting from a base that already has those loops skips the sculpt and the rebuild.
- **Separate pieces:** stylized characters for games ship as a head, a body, hair, clothing and
  footwear as separate meshes, with teeth, eyes and lashes separate again
  ([Epic, MetaHuman stylized assembly](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-stylized-character-final-assembly),
  [MetaHuman stylized geometry and rig](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-stylized-character-character-geometry-and-rig-creation),
  [a modular character workflow](https://www.renderhub.com/blog/inside-ryanreos-modular-game-ready-3d-character-workflow)).
  Pixar's own pipeline has a paper on separate facial features
  ([Eyes Without a Face](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/DFF/paper.pdf)).
- **Faces by blendshapes:** a stylized base mesh ships with blink, mouth-open and smile shapes.
  three.js plays them as morph targets: lip sync is writing weights to them over time
  ([lip sync in three.js](https://www.abratabia.com/ai-talking-characters/lip-sync-in-threejs.php)).
  Speech-only sets are visemes (about 15 shapes); ARKit's 52 cover the whole face
  ([Convai](https://convai.com/blog/lip-syncing-virtual-ai-characters-techniques-integration-and-future-trends)).
  Bought stylized bases advertise the same thing: a rig plus 90 or so face shapes
  ([an example](https://superhivemarket.com/products/stylized-male--female-basemesh-bundle-duo--rigged-with-90-blendshapes)).
- **Lip sync from recordings:** Rhubarb Lip Sync turns a WAV and its text into a timeline of six to
  nine mouth shapes
  ([rhubarb-lip-sync](https://github.com/wzpan/rhubarb-lip-sync)).
- **Hair:** chains of spring bones down clumps of hair, kept out of the head by sphere colliders, as
  VRM characters do: a few dozen points a character, far cheaper than cloth
  ([VRM spring bones](https://vrm.dev/en/univrm/springbone/univrm_secondary/),
  [VirtualCast](https://wiki.virtualcast.jp/wiki/en/vrm/setting/spring)).
- **MakeHuman from scripts:** MPFB's own sample scripts make a person, add a rig, eyes, brows,
  lashes, tongue and teeth, and export, all by code
  ([samples](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/tree/master/script_samples)).
- **Other free bases looked at:** Quaternius's Universal Base Characters (CC0, about 13,000
  triangles, made for games) are stylized and rigged, but nothing says they have face shapes or
  finger bones ([pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalbasecharacters.html)).
- **Rigging in Blender:** Rigify makes a full rig with a face, but exports only its deform bones,
  with at most four weights a vertex, and drivers don't survive
  ([Faceit export notes](https://faceit-doc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/export/)).
- **Animations:** Mixamo's are free for games but can't be published as raw files, and this repo is
  public ([Adobe's FAQ](https://community.adobe.com/t5/mixamo-discussions/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos/td-p/13234775)).
  Quaternius's Universal Animation Library is CC0
  ([guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-character-animations-rigging)).
- **Triangle budgets:** published numbers for a phone's main character run from about 20,000 to
  40,000 ([Game-Ace](https://game-ace.com/blog/3d-character-modeling/)) down to a few thousand
  ([Pixune](https://pixune.com/blog/high-poly-to-low-poly-workflow-in-game-art/)), depending on how
  close the camera comes. Hence two copies.

## Built so far (2026-11-14)

- Every finger and the thumb have three bones on the Medic, found on the model by where its
  vertices lie across the hand. A hand closes a finger at a time, little finger first, with the
  thumb folding across, and can point. The other heroes go through `node scripts/rig-hero.mjs
  <hero>` the same way, each to be looked at after.
- Spring-bone hair (`render/springBones.ts`): built and working, waiting for hair that's its own
  piece.
- Blinking lids, lower lids, eyes that dart and look, and brows that move with the mood
  (`render/heroAnimator.ts`): written, not yet seen on a model, since no model has those pieces.
- A jaw added to every hero as it loads (`riggedHeroes.ts`), opening with the words. On the fused
  shells it opens only a crack.
- Mouths keep time with the line in cutscenes, from its words (`render/lipSync.ts`).
- Stand-in figures for the story's people (`storyFigure` in `render/heroModels.ts`), with a mouth
  that opens. Cutscenes use these, and the Rifleman's body for the Captain, until the new models
  exist.
